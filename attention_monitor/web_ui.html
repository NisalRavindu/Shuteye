    In short
    
      
    The monitor watches your face through the webcam and decides, many times a second, whether you're looking at your study material. If you look away, close your eyes or leave for longer than a short grace period, it beeps. At the end it tells you how much time was actually lost. Brief, harmless breaks aren't counted.
    
      
    Numbers in bold on this page are your current settings. Change them under the ⚙ Settings button at the top.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Study days
    
      
      
        
    • Start day follows today's timetable: it shows the current subject, the time left in it and what's next. The camera only watches you during your subjects (and catch-up time). Breaks and free time aren't monitored.
      • 
        
    • Automatic breaks (0–5 min, set in the Timetable toolbar) are taken from the end of a subject that's directly followed by another one. The next subject still starts on time. They aren't shown in the grid.
      • 
        
    • Take a break whenever you like, for as long as you like. The camera switches off and it isn't counted as wasted, but the subject's clock keeps running, so it counts against today's goal.
      • 
        
    • Missing time (wasted, own breaks, late start) is suggested back to you in today's free slots. Press Start now in a free slot to catch up on that subject.
      • 
        
    • The study day ends at the time set in the Timetable toolbar (default 04:00 next morning), not at midnight, so a late session still counts for the day it started on. If you forget to end the day, or fall asleep, it closes automatically at that time, even if this app was closed in between. A planned day you never start counts as fully missed.
      • 
        
    • When a day ends, the Analysis tab opens with where the time went, per subject. Switch to Week for the whole week.
      • 
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Calibration: 2 positions, about 12 seconds
    
      
      
        
    • Step 1: look at your screen the way you normally study. After about 3 s you hear a beep: close your eyes and keep them closed until the next beep. You don't move, and the beeps tell you when, so you never need to read the screen with your eyes shut.
      • 
        
    • Step 2: look down at your book or paper as if writing.
      • 
      
    
      
    Everything later is compared to you in these positions. Any head position between screen and book, plus a margin, counts as focused. The closed-eye sample teaches it what your closed eyes look like, so it doesn't rely on a one-size-fits-all threshold. If you move the camera or your chair, press Recalibrate.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    What it watches
    
      
    
        
    SignalHow it's measuredUsed to detect
    
        
    Your faceA face-landmark model finds 478 points on your face in each frame.Not at camera
    
        
    Head angleHow far your head is turned left/right and tilted up/down, compared to the range between your two calibrated positions.Looking away
    
        
    Eye directionWhere your iris sits between the corners of your eye. Sideways only: up/down eye movement is normal when reading, so it's ignored.Looking away (eyes drifting to the side while your head stays still)
    
        
    EyelidsTwo signals combined: the model's blink score and the measured gap between your lids. Compared to your own open and closed eyes in each position, so lowered lids while writing don't count as closed.Eyes closed
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    What triggers an alert
    
      
    
        
    StateMeansAlert after
    
        
    Looking away
            Head turned more than 20° or tilted more than 15° beyond your calibrated range, or eyes off to the side.
            4 s
    
        
    Eyes closed
            Eyes shut (eyes-closed score at or above 1.00, shown live on the Monitor tab). Normal blinks are far too short to count.
            3 s
    
        
    Not at camera
            No face found: you left, turned fully around, or something is covering your face.

    Restless
            4 quick breaks in a row, each starting within 30 s of the previous one. This catches the in-out-in-out pattern of breaks that each stay just under the timers above.
            at once
    
      
    
      
    While you stay distracted it repeats the alert every 8 s. Each kind of alert has its own beep pattern, so you can tell them apart without looking.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    What it forgives
    
      
      
        
    • Glances. Anything shorter than the alert times above never alerts.
      • 
        
    • Flicker. You must be back for 0.8 s before a break counts as over. Looking back for a split second doesn't reset the timer, and it doesn't split one break into many either.
      • 
        
    • Noise. Breaks under 1 s are treated as detection noise and dropped completely.
      • 
        
    • Short isolated breaks under 10 s (grabbing a pen, a stretch) are not counted as wasted time. They're listed separately as "ignored".
      • 
        
    • Breaks you take. In a study day, press Take a break; in a quick session, Pause. That time isn't counted as wasted.
      • 
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    How wasted time and focus % are scored
    
      
      
        
    • A break of 10 s or more counts as wasted, in full.
      • 
        
    • A shorter break counts only if it's part of a restless spell. Then every break in that spell counts.
      • 
        
    • Focus % = 100% − wasted time ÷ tracked time. Calibration and paused time aren't tracked.
      • 
        
    • A CSV log of every alert and break is saved in the sessions folder.
      • 
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    What it's sensitive to
    
      
      
        
    • Lighting. A dim room or a bright window behind you can make it lose your face (counted as "not at camera") and makes eye readings noisy. Light from the front works best.
      • 
        
    • Glasses. Screen glare on lenses can hide your eyes. If you get false eyes-closed or "eyes looking left/right" alerts, recalibrate, or turn off Track eye direction under Sensitivity.
      • 
        
    • Covering your face. Hair over your eyes, a cap, or resting your chin or cheek on your hand can hide landmarks.
      • 
        
    • Moving after calibration. Moving the laptop, camera or chair, or leaning far back, shifts all the angles. Press Recalibrate.
      • 
        
    • Material outside the calibrated range. A second monitor or notes off to the side will count as looking away. Raise Head turn allowed.
      • 
        
    • Other people. It follows one face. Someone else walking through the frame can briefly confuse it.
      • 
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    What it can't tell
    
      
      
        
    • Whether your mind is on the material. Daydreaming while staring at your book looks focused.
      • 
        
    • What's on your screen. Notes and a video look the same from the camera.
      • 
        
    • Listening to a lecture with your eyes closed counts as eyes closed. Pause it for that.
      • 
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Privacy
    
      
    Everything runs on this computer. The video is never recorded or sent anywhere, and the page is only reachable from this machine. The session log contains only times and event types, no images.
